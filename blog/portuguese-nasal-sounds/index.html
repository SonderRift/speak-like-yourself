How to Pronounce the Nasal Sounds (ão, ãe, õe)

A step-by-step way into the sound that gives most beginners away, with audio so you can match it out loud.

The Portuguese nasal sounds are the single feature that most marks you as a beginner, and the fastest fix for your accent. English has nothing quite like ão in não, so your ear has to build it from scratch. Good news: once you feel where the sound goes, it clicks. This guide walks through ão, ãe and õe with audio and a trick you can try right now.

What makes a vowel nasal

A nasal vowel just means the air escapes through your nose instead of only your mouth. In Portuguese this happens with the tilde (ã, õ) and whenever a vowel is followed by m or n at the end of a syllable (bem, sim, um).

Try it now

Say the vowel but don't fully close your mouth, let it hum out your nose. Pinch your nose and say não: if the sound changes and your nose vibrates, you're nasalizing correctly.

The -ão sound (the big one)

ão is a nasal glide, roughly “ah” sliding into “oo,” all through the nose. It is not “now” and not “owww.” Practice the family: não (no), pão (bread), mão (hand), irmão (brother). Bonus: many plurals turn ão into ões, so coração (heart) becomes corações.

The -ãe and -õe sounds

ãe is a nasal “ai” (as in “eye”) through the nose: mãe (mother), pães (breads). õe is a nasal “oy”: põe (puts), limões (lemons). They are less common than ão but follow the same nose-first logic. Hear them above and copy out loud.

Daily practice that works

Pick one nasal a day and say its word family ten times out loud, exaggerating the nose. Record yourself and compare to the audio. These sounds live in the fuller pronunciation guide, and they pair with the Brazilian R as the two accent upgrades worth obsessing over first.

Frequently asked questions

How do you pronounce ão in Portuguese?

It's a nasal glide from "ah" toward "oo," sent through your nose, as in não and pão. Pinch your nose and say não: if the sound changes, you're doing it right.

Why are nasal sounds so hard for English speakers?

English has no true nasal vowels, so your ear and mouth have to learn the sound from scratch. With a few days of out-loud practice it becomes natural. Hear examples above.

When is a vowel nasal in Portuguese?

When it has a tilde (ã, õ) or is followed by m or n at the end of a syllable, like bem, sim, um. See the full pronunciation guide.
