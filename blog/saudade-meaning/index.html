What Does 'Saudade' Really Mean? (The Untranslatable Word)

What does saudade really mean? A Brazilian teacher explains the untranslatable word for longing, how to use it, and why it says so much about Brazil.

Rafael · Brazilian Portuguese teacherAugust 11, 2026 · 2 min read

If you searched for saudade meaning, you’re in the right place. If you only learn one “untranslatable” Portuguese word, make it this one. Saudade is the bittersweet ache of missing someone or something you love, a longing wrapped in tenderness. English has no single word for it, which is exactly why Brazilians are a little proud of it. Let me explain what saudade really means, how to use it, and why it opens a window into the Brazilian soul.

So what does saudade actually mean?

Saudade is the feeling of missing something: a person, a place, a time in your life, even a version of yourself. But it is not pure sadness. It carries warmth, the sweetness of having loved something enough to miss it. Think of the feeling when a song reminds you of someone far away, and you smile and ache at the same time. That is saudade.

How to use saudade in a sentence

The most important pattern is estar com saudade(s) de (to be missing someone/something).

Portuguese English
Estou com saudade de você. I miss you.
Que saudade! I miss it/them so much! (as an exclamation)
Estou morrendo de saudade de você. I’m dying of missing you (very Brazilian intensity).
Bater a saudade When the longing hits you
Matar a saudade To “kill” the longing, to reunite or revisit

Saudade and saudades: is there a difference?

You will hear both the singular saudade and the plural saudades, and honestly they are used almost interchangeably in everyday Brazilian speech. Saudades can feel a touch more affectionate, which is why people sign off messages with it. Do not overthink it.

Why this one word says so much about Brazil

Saudade runs through Brazilian music, from bossa nova to sertanejo, and through the way people talk about home, family and love. It reflects a culture that feels deeply and says so out loud. Learn to feel saudade and you understand something textbooks cannot teach. It pairs beautifully with the warmth in Brazilian terms of endearment and the everyday slang that colors real conversation.

The takeaway

Saudade is more than vocabulary, it is a feeling that captures how Brazilians love and remember. Use estou com saudade the next time you miss someone, and you will sound, and feel, a little more Brazilian. Ready to keep collecting the words and phrases that make you sound truly Brazilian? Frequently asked questions

Is saudade really untranslatable?

There is no single English word that captures it. "Longing" or "missing" get close but lose the bittersweet, affectionate warmth. That gap is why Brazilians treasure the word.

How do you say "I miss you" in Brazilian Portuguese?

Estou com saudade de você, or more casually tô com saudade de você. For extra Brazilian intensity: estou morrendo de saudade.

Saudade or saudades, which is correct?

Both are correct and used interchangeably in Brazil. Saudades can feel slightly warmer, common in sign-offs. See more heartfelt words in [terms of endearment](/speak-like-yourself/blog/portuguese-terms-of-endearment/).
