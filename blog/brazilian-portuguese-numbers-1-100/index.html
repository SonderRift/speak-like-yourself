The tens, and building everything in between

The trick is simple: from 21 onward, take the tens word (vinte, trinta, quarenta, and so on), add e (“and”), then the unit (1-9). So 21 is vinte e um, 34 is trinta e quatro, 99 is noventa e nove.

Two rules beginners miss

First, gender: um and dois are the only numbers that change for feminine nouns, becoming uma and duas. So it is um carro but uma casa, dois reais but duas horas. Every other number stays the same, whatever the gender. Second, cem vs cento: exactly 100 is cem, but 101 to 199 use cento: cento e um (101), cento e cinquenta (150), cento e cinquenta e um (151). Small rules, but natives notice.

Frequently asked questions

How do you count to 100 in Brazilian Portuguese?

Learn 1 to 20 and the tens (vinte, trinta...), then combine with e: 21 is vinte e um, 45 is quarenta e cinco. Exactly 100 is cem. Hear them in the audio above.

What's the difference between cem and cento?

Cem is exactly 100. For 101 to 199 you use cento: cento e um (101), cento e cinquenta (150).

Do numbers change for gender in Portuguese?

Only um/uma (one) and dois/duas (two): um carro but uma casa, dois dias but duas horas. The rest stay the same.
