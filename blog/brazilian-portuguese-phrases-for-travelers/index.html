101 Brazilian Portuguese Phrases for Travelers

101 essential Brazilian Portuguese phrases for travelers, greetings, directions, restaurants, shopping and emergencies, with pronunciation and audio samples.

If you’re traveling to Brazil, these Brazilian Portuguese phrases are the ones that actually matter, the stuff you’ll use at the airport, in a restaurant, in a taxi, and when something goes wrong. Brazilians are famously warm, and even a few words in Portuguese changes how you’re treated. You don’t need grammar for this trip; you need phrases you can say with confidence. Here are 101 of them, organized by situation, with a note on pronunciation.

Want to hear them out loud? A few are spoken in the audio samples below so you can copy them right away. The full audio, all 101 phrases at natural speed to shadow, lives in the course. Start with the free printable PDF, and get the complete audio in the store when you want to train your ear.

Greetings and being polite

Start here. Brazilians open almost everything with warmth, lead with a smile and one of these. For the full set, see Brazilian greetings.

Portuguese English
Oi / Olá Hi / Hello
Bom dia Good morning
Boa tarde Good afternoon
Boa noite Good evening / good night
Tudo bem? How are you? (lit. all good?)
Tudo bem! I’m good! (also the answer)
Obrigado / Obrigada Thank you (man / woman speaking)
De nada You’re welcome
Por favor Please
Com licença Excuse me (to pass / get attention)
Desculpa Sorry
Tchau Bye
Até logo See you soon
Prazer Nice to meet you

Emergencies, the ones you hope you never need

Say these slowly and clearly.

Good to know

In an emergency in Brazil, dial 190 for police and 192 for an ambulance.

Portuguese English

Estou doente I’m sick

Eu perdi meu passaporte I lost my passport
Fui roubado / roubada I was robbed (man / woman)
Onde fica a delegacia? Where’s the police station?
Onde fica o hospital mais próximo? Where’s the nearest hospital?
É uma emergência It’s an emergency

Frequently asked questions

Do I say obrigado or obrigada?

Match it to your own gender: a man says obrigado, a woman says obrigada, regardless of who you're thanking.

Is a little Portuguese really worth it in Brazil?

Absolutely. English isn't widely spoken outside tourist hubs, and Brazilians respond warmly to any effort. Even oi, tudo bem? changes the interaction.

How do I remember all 101 phrases?

Don't try to. Pick the 15 for situations you'll actually hit, say them out loud daily, and download the free PDF so they're on your phone. The rest you'll pick up as you go, the beginner roadmap shows the order.
