How Brazilians Text: WhatsApp Slang & Abbreviations (kkk, vc, tb)

Brazilian Portuguese texting slang decoded: kkk, vc, tb, blz, vlw and the WhatsApp abbreviations Brazilians actually type, explained by a native teacher.

Rafael · Brazilian Portuguese teacherAugust 12, 2026 · 4 min read

Open any Brazilian’s phone and you’ll see it: the language they text is not the language in your app. It’s faster, warmer, and full of shortcuts no course teaches. This is Brazilian Portuguese texting slang, the WhatsApp shorthand that runs real friendships in Brazil, from kkk to vc to vlw. Learn to read it and you stop being the friend everyone has to slow down for. Here’s what Brazilians actually type, and when to type it back.

First, why this matters so much in Brazil

WhatsApp (or zap, as everyone calls it) is not just an app in Brazil, it’s how the country talks. Plans, work, family, flirting, gossip: almost all of it happens in those green bubbles. That means the informal, sound-it-out, drop-the-vowels way Brazilians text is not lazy, it’s the real register of the language among friends. If you only ever meet Portuguese in a classroom, this whole layer stays invisible. It’s the written cousin of the spoken slang that makes you sound like a person instead of an app.

The laugh: kkk, rs, and how long is too long

Brazilians do not type “lol”. They type kkk or hahaha. It comes from the sound of laughing (ca-ca-ca / ra-ra-ra), and the length is the volume:

You type It means
kkkk / kkkkkk A real laugh, genuinely funny
kk A polite or flat laugh (careful, can read as cold)
rs / rsrs Risos, a softer, gentler chuckle (a bit older-school)
haha / hahaha Very common among Brazilians too
KKKKK / HAHAHAHA All caps mean it is super funny

The rule Brazilians never say out loud but all feel: a lonely kk after your joke can sting a little, like a courtesy laugh. When something is actually funny, let the k’s or ha’s run.

The everyday shortcuts

These are the workhorses. Once your eye knows them, most casual messages become readable at a glance.

Short Full English
vc / cê você you
vcs vocês you all
tb / tbm também also / too
q que that / what
pq porque / por quê because / why
blz beleza cool / all good
td tudo everything (td bem? = all good?)
mt / mto muito very / a lot
tá / tô está / estou it is / I am
hj hoje today
fds fim de semana weekend
msg mensagem message
cmg / ctg comigo / contigo with me / with you

Beleza is a whole story on its own, by the way: see beleza, legal, massa for how Brazilians say “cool.”

Warmth and sign-offs

Brazilian messages are affectionate, even between friends. The goodbye carries the warmth:

Short Full English
vlw valeu thanks! / cheers
flw falou later / see ya (often paired: vlw, flw)
obg / obgd obrigado / obrigada thank you
bj / bjs / bjo beijo / beijos kiss / kisses (a normal, friendly sign-off)
abs abraços hugs
pfv / pf por favor please

Signing off a message to a friend with bjs or abs is completely normal and not romantic by default; it’s just how Brazilians greet each other with a kiss and/or a hug, but in text form. It sits right next to the way Brazilians greet each other in person.

Slang that lives mostly online

A few expressions you’ll meet more in text than in speech:

  • mds = meu Deus (OMG)
  • vdd = verdade (true / for real)
  • sqn = só que não (the sarcastic “not!”, tacked onto a fake statement)
  • tlgd = tá ligado? (you know? / you feel me?)
  • glr = galera (the crew / everyone)
  • dps = depois (later / after)
  • partiu = let’s go / I’m in (partiu praia? = beach?)

A quick note on tone

Two things save you from sounding off. First, mirror the other person: if they write full words, you write full words; if they fire off vc and kkk, match it. Second, keep it for the right rooms. Texting slang is for friends and family, not a message to your landlord, a new boss, or someone you just met. There, spell things out. Read the room and you’ll never misfire. This is exactly the instinct the beginner roadmap is built to grow.

The takeaway

Brazilian texting slang isn’t a corruption of the language, it’s the friendliest version of it. Start by reading: next time a Brazilian friend sends kkk vc vai? blz, flw, you’ll know they laughed, asked if you’re coming, said cool, and signed off. Frequently asked questions

What does kkk mean in Brazilian Portuguese texting?

It's laughter, the Brazilian version of "haha." The k's come from ca-ca-ca, the sound of laughing. More k's means a bigger laugh, so kkkkk is a real belly laugh and a flat kk can read as cold or sarcastic. Older or softer texters write rs (short for risos) instead.

What does vc mean in Portuguese?

vc is short for você (you), one of the most common abbreviations in Brazilian texting. vcs is vocês (you all). You'll also see cê, which is how você actually sounds out loud.

Is it OK to use texting slang when I write to Brazilians?

Yes, with friends. WhatsApp is where Brazilians are most informal, so vc, tb, blz and kkk are completely normal and warm. Keep full spelling for work messages and people you don't know yet. When in doubt, mirror how the other person is typing.
