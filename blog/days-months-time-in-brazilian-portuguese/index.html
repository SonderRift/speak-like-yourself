Days, Months & Telling Time in Brazilian Portuguese

Days of the week, months and telling time in Brazilian Portuguese: full reference tables, how dates work, and the little habits that trip beginners up.

Rafael · Brazilian Portuguese teacherAugust 11, 2026 · 2 min read

The days of the week in Portuguese look intimidating at first (why is Tuesday called the “third fair”?), but there is a simple logic once you see it. This is your reference for days, months, and telling time in Brazilian Portuguese, with the small habits, like how Brazilians write dates and use the 24-hour clock, that textbooks skip.

The days of the week

Here is the trick: the weekdays are numbered, not named. Counted from Sunday, the first day of the week, Monday becomes segunda-feira (“second”), Tuesday terça-feira (“third”), and on through Friday. In casual speech, people usually drop -feira and just say segunda, terça, and so on. For the curious, -feira means “fair,” from the markets held on holy days: Monday, the second day of the week, was the first market day, then the count runs on to sexta-feira, Friday. Only Monday to Friday take -feira: the weekend kept its own names, sábado from the Sabbath and domingo from dies Dominica, the Lord’s day.

Portuguese English
domingo Sunday
segunda-feira (segunda) Monday
terça-feira (terça) Tuesday
quarta-feira (quarta) Wednesday
quinta-feira (quinta) Thursday
sexta-feira (sexta) Friday
sábado Saturday

The months

Months are lowercase in Portuguese and close to their English cousins.

Portuguese English
janeiro / fevereiro / março January / February / March
abril / maio / junho April / May / June
julho / agosto / setembro July / August / September
outubro / novembro / dezembro October / November / December

Telling time

Use são for most hours and é only for one o’clock, noon and midnight. Brazilians mix the 12-hour clock in speech with the 24-hour clock in writing and schedules (14h = 2 PM).

Portuguese English
Que horas são? What time is it?
É uma hora It’s one o’clock
São duas horas It’s two o’clock
São três e meia It’s three thirty
São quatro e quinze It’s four fifteen
meio-dia / meia-noite noon / midnight
da manhã / da tarde / da noite in the morning / afternoon / evening

Dates and useful time words

Brazilians write dates day/month/year (05/07 is July 5th, not May 7th), and say dia cinco de julho. A few words you will use daily: hoje (today), amanhã (tomorrow), ontem (yesterday), agora (now), mais tarde (later), cedo (early), tarde (late). Practicing these out loud is great rhythm training, and pairs well with the numbers 1 to 100 guide.

Frequently asked questions

Why are the days of the week in Portuguese called feira?

Counted from Sunday, the first day of the week, the weekdays are numbered and carry feira (fair), from the markets held on holy days: Monday, the second day, was the first market day, segunda-feira, on through sexta-feira, Friday. The weekend kept its own names, sábado from the Sabbath and domingo from dies Dominica, the Lord's day. In speech, people often drop -feira.

How do you tell time in Brazilian Portuguese?

Ask que horas são? Use é for one o'clock, noon and midnight, and são for the rest: são três e meia is 3:30. Writing and schedules often use the 24-hour clock.

How do Brazilians write dates?

Day/month/year. So 05/07 means July 5th. Spoken: dia cinco de julho. Months are written in lowercase.
