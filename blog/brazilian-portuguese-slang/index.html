Brazilian Slang: 50 Gírias to Sound Like a Native

50 real Brazilian slang words (gírias) that make you sound like a native, everyday expressions, reactions and cool-talk, explained by a Brazilian teacher.

Rafael · Brazilian Portuguese teacherAugust 11, 2026 · 5 min read

Here’s the truth no textbook tells you: Brazilians don’t talk like textbooks. If you want to sound natural, you need Brazilian Portuguese slang, the gírias that flavor every real conversation. Learn these and you stop sounding like a language app and start sounding like a person. Here are 50 of the most useful, from a Brazilian who uses them every day, with when (and when not) to say them.

First, what is a gíria?

Gíria just means “slang”, the informal words friends use with each other. Brazilian Portuguese is extremely informal in daily life, much more than the grammar books suggest. You’ll hear these on the street, in music, in messages, in novelas. They’re not rude; they’re the temperature of real speech. A quick note: slang is regional, so a few of these are stronger in Rio or São Paulo. I’ll flag the big ones.

The everyday essentials (use these first)

Portuguese English
Beleza Cool / alright / deal, also “you good?”
Legal Cool / nice (the most useful word in Brazil)
Massa Awesome (very common in the Northeast & among young people)
Cara Dude / guy (São Paulo’s “man”)
Mano Bro / man
Valeu Thanks! / cheers (casual obrigado)
Nossa! Wow! / oh my (short for nossa senhora)
Caramba! Wow / dang (safe, family-friendly)
Tá / Tá bom OK / alright
Firmeza All good / solid (street slang)

How to say something is ‘cool’

Brazil has a lot of ways to say cool, and the choice signals where you’re from and how old you are. Full breakdown in beleza, legal, massa.

Portuguese English
Que legal! How cool!
Que massa! How awesome!
Show / Show de bola Great / awesome
Maneiro Cool (very Rio)
Irado Sick / awesome
Da hora Cool (São Paulo)
Top Top-notch / great

Reactions and filler words

These make you sound fluent because they buy you thinking time the way “like” and “you know” do in English.

Portuguese English
Tipo… Like… (filler)
Então… So… / well…
Sei lá I dunno / whatever
Pois é Yeah, exactly / that’s the thing
Né? Right? (tag on the end of anything)
Ué Huh? / wait, what? (surprise)
Eita! Whoa! / yikes!
Affff Ugh (exasperation)
Que saco! How annoying! (lit. what a bag)
Poxa Aw / dang (mild disappointment)

Crush Crush (yes, borrowed from English)

A grab bag of gírias that come up constantly once you’re living the day to day, at work, out with friends, or just texting.

Grana Money / cash

Slang to use carefully

A few words you’ll hear constantly but should handle with awareness of context. None of these are extreme, but they’re informal enough that you’d keep them among friends, not in a job interview.

Mano/mina (bro/girl), cara de pau (shameless, lit. “wooden face”), pagar mico (to embarrass yourself), dar um perdido (to ghost someone), encher o saco (to annoy / bug someone). Listen for how Brazilians use them before you deploy them, tone matters more than the dictionary.

The most Brazilian word of all

If there’s one word that captures the Brazilian soul, it’s not slang exactly. It’s saudade, that bittersweet longing for someone or something you love and miss. It’s so central that it gets its own story: what saudade really means.

The takeaway

Slang is what turns correct Portuguese into real Portuguese. Start with the ten everyday essentials, legal, beleza, valeu, nossa, cara, and sprinkle them in until they feel natural. Frequently asked questions

Is Brazilian slang rude?

Most everyday gírias (legal, beleza, massa, valeu) are completely friendly and safe. Brazil is just a very informal culture, slang is warmth, not disrespect. A handful are context-sensitive, which I flag above.

Will slang from Rio work in São Paulo?

Mostly yes, but some words are regional, maneiro is very Rio, da hora is very São Paulo, massa is strong in the Northeast. Everyone understands all of them, though.

How do I practice slang without sounding forced?

Learn them from real input, music, YouTube, novelas, so you absorb the tone, not just the word. Start with the 10 everyday essentials and let the rest come. The [beginner roadmap](/speak-like-yourself/blog/how-to-learn-brazilian-portuguese/) shows where slang fits in.
