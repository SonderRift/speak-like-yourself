Brazilian Portuguese Greetings: Say Hello Like a Local

How to say hello in Brazilian Portuguese like a local: real greetings for morning, night, texting and meeting people, with pronunciation and cultural notes.

Rafael · Brazilian Portuguese teacherAugust 11, 2026 · 2 min read

Learning how to say hello in Brazilian Portuguese is the fastest way to win people over, because Brazilians greet warmly and notice immediately when you try. There is more to it than olá. Here are the greetings locals actually use, when to use each, and the small cultural touches that make you sound natural instead of like a phrasebook.

The everyday greetings

Start here. Oi is the casual, all-day “hi,” and tudo bem? doubles as both “how are you?” and, with an affirmative tone, “I’m good.”

Portuguese English
Oi! Hi! (casual, most common)
Olá! Hello (a touch more formal)
Tudo bem? How are you? (lit. all good?)
Tudo bem! / Tudo ótimo! I’m good! / Great!
E aí, beleza? What’s up? (very casual, with friends)
Como vai? How’s it going?

Greetings by time of day

Brazilians switch these based on the clock. Boa noite covers both “good evening” and “good night.”

Portuguese English
Bom dia Good morning (until noon)
Boa tarde Good afternoon (noon to sunset)
Boa noite Good evening / good night

Meeting someone for the first time

When you meet someone, prazer (a pleasure) is the go-to. A quick cultural note: Brazilians are physically warm. Women usually greet with a cheek kiss (one or two, depending on the region), and men often shake hands or, among friends, do a quick hug. When in doubt, follow their lead. Learn a little slang too, and you will blend right in.

Saying goodbye

Portuguese English
Tchau! Bye! (most common)
Até logo See you soon
Até amanhã See you tomorrow
Falou! / Valeu! Later! / Cheers! (very casual)
Um abraço A hug (warm sign-off, even in texts; men use it with other men)
Um beijo A kiss (warm sign-off, even in texts; used man to woman or woman to woman)

The takeaway

Nailing your greetings is the quickest confidence win in Brazilian Portuguese, and Brazilians will love you for trying. Practice oi, tudo bem? until it is automatic, then add the rest. Grab the free phrase list below to keep them in your pocket.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most common greeting in Brazil?

Oi for hello and tchau for goodbye, usually with tudo bem? right after. They work in almost any casual situation.

Do Brazilians really kiss on the cheek to say hello?

Often, yes, especially women greeting others. The number of kisses varies by region. Men more often shake hands or hug. Follow the other person's lead and you will be fine.

How do I greet someone politely in a message?

Oi, tudo bem? opens almost any text warmly. Sign off with um abraço for a friendly touch. The free [travel phrases](/speak-like-yourself/blog/brazilian-portuguese-phrases-for-travelers/) list has more.
