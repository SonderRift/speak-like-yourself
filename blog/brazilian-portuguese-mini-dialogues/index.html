Survival & Travel Phrases

Brazilian Portuguese Mini Dialogues for Real Life

Short, ready-to-use exchanges for greetings, ordering, asking for help, and keeping a simple conversation going.

RafaelBrazilian Portuguese teacher · August 27, 2026 · 8 min read

These Brazilian Portuguese dialogues give you the rhythm of real conversation in bite-sized pieces you can memorize and reuse. Exchanges in Brazil are short: someone greets you, you answer, they ask one thing, you reply. If you know the words but freeze the moment someone answers back, this is the missing piece. Read each one for meaning, say it out loud, then swap one detail to make it yours.

How to use these dialogues

Do not just read them with your eyes. Say both parts out loud, even the other person’s lines, because that trains your ear to expect the answer. Then change one thing: the food, the place, the time, or the name. That small swap is what turns a memorized script into flexible speech.

Step What you do
1. Read Understand the meaning once
2. Repeat Say both lines out loud
3. Swap Change one detail and say it again

Dialogue 1: A quick greeting

This is the one you will use most. Keep it light and warm.

A: Oi, tudo bem? (Hi, how are you?)
B: Tudo bem, e você? (I’m good, and you?)
A: Tudo ótimo, obrigado. (All great, thank you.)

Swap idea: answer with mais ou menos (so-so) instead of tudo bem and see how the tone changes. For the full range of hellos and goodbyes, see Brazilian Portuguese greetings.

Dialogue 2: Ordering at a café

Short, polite, done.

A: Bom dia! Um cafezinho, por favor. (Good morning! A little coffee, please.)
B: Claro. Mais alguma coisa? (Of course. Anything else?)
A: Só isso, obrigado. Quanto é? (That’s all, thank you. How much is it?)
B: Seis reais. (Six reais.)

Swap idea: replace um cafezinho with uma água (a water) or um pão de queijo (a cheese bread). The rest of the dialogue stays the same. When you want the full restaurant playbook, read how to order food in Brazil.

Dialogue 3: Asking for help when you are lost

You will not catch every word of the answer, and that is fine. The goal is to keep the exchange going.

A: Com licença, onde fica o banheiro? (Excuse me, where is the bathroom?)
B: Fica ali, à direita. (It’s over there, on the right.)
A: Desculpa, pode repetir mais devagar? (Sorry, can you repeat more slowly?)
B: À direita, no fundo. (On the right, at the back.)
A: Ah, entendi. Obrigado! (Ah, I got it. Thank you!)

The two lines that save you here are pode repetir? and mais devagar (can you repeat / more slowly). Keep them ready. For getting around a new city, the airport and transport phrases fit right in with this.

Dialogue 4: A small purchase

Friendly back-and-forth, nothing complicated.

A: Oi, quanto custa esta camiseta? (Hi, how much is this t-shirt?)
B: Quarenta reais. (Forty reais.)
A: Tem em outra cor? (Do you have it in another color?)
B: Tenho azul e preto. (I have blue and black.)
A: Vou levar a azul. Aceita cartão? (I’ll take the blue one. Do you take card?)

Swap idea: change camiseta to any item and azul to any color. The structure carries you through most shops and market stalls.

Dialogue 5: Keeping a simple conversation alive

Brazilians love that you are trying, so a little small talk goes a long way. These questions keep things moving without needing advanced grammar.

A: De onde você é? (Where are you from?)
B: Eu sou dos Estados Unidos. E você? (I’m from the United States. And you?)
A: Sou daqui, de São Paulo. É a sua primeira vez no Brasil? (I’m from here, São Paulo. Is it your first time in Brazil?)
B: É sim. Estou adorando! (Yes it is. I’m loving it!)

Notice how each answer ends with a small question back: e você? (and you?). That one habit keeps a conversation alive longer than any big vocabulary list.

The phrases that hold any dialogue together

No matter the situation, these connectors buy you time and keep you polite while you think.

Portuguese English
Desculpa Sorry
Pode repetir? Can you repeat?
Mais devagar, por favor More slowly, please
Como assim? What do you mean?
Entendi Got it
Deixa eu ver Let me see
E você? And you?
Só isso That’s all

Watch out for words that look English but are not, because they trip up a lot of learners mid-conversation. A quick read of Brazilian Portuguese false friends will keep you from a few common slips.

Teacher's note

You will not catch every word of the reply, and that is completely fine. A dialogue is not a listening test, it is a back-and-forth you keep alive. Nod, lean on pode repetir?, and let the rest wash over you. The person in front of you wants the conversation to work just as much as you do.

You do not need a big vocabulary to have a real conversation. You need a few patterns you can say without thinking, plus one question to hand back.

The takeaway

Pick two of these dialogues that match where you are going and drill them until they are automatic. Say both parts, swap one detail, and repeat tomorrow. That is the whole method, and it works far better than memorizing word lists alone. Frequently asked questions

Are mini dialogues good for learning Portuguese?

Yes. Mini dialogues teach vocabulary, rhythm, and response patterns together, which is closer to real speaking than isolated word lists.

How should I practice these dialogues?

Read once for meaning, repeat out loud, then swap one detail such as the food, place, time, or greeting.
