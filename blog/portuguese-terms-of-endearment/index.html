Brazilian Portuguese Terms of Endearment (amor, meu bem...)

Brazilian Portuguese terms of endearment: sweet names for partners, family and friends like amor, meu bem and querido, and how to use them naturally.

Rafael · Brazilian Portuguese teacherAugust 11, 2026 · 2 min read

Brazilians are warm, and it shows in their language. Portuguese terms of endearment are used generously, not just with partners but with family, friends, and even the person at the bakery. Learning a few makes you sound affectionate and natural, the way Brazilians actually talk. Here are the sweetest / cheesy ones, who they are for, and the little grammar trick that turns almost any word tender.

For a partner or crush

Portuguese English
amor love (by far the most common, used even casually)
meu amor my love
vida / minha vida my life (deeply affectionate)
meu bem my dear (lit. my good)
benzinho sweetie (tender diminutive of bem)
gato / gata hottie (lit. cat)
xodó sweetheart, someone you treasure (very cheesy)

For family and friends

Endearment is not only romantic. Parents call kids filho/filha (son/daughter) affectionately even when grown, and friends toss around sweet names freely.

Portuguese English
filho / filha son / daughter (warm, any age)
filhote / filhota little one (lit. a young animal, said fondly to a kid)
meu velho / minha velha my old man / old lady (fond, for a parent or longtime friend)
mano / mana bro / sis (slang / warm)
querido / querida dear (warm and friendly)
fofo / fofa cutie
lindo / linda good-looking

The -inho trick that sweetens everything

Add -inho or -inha to a word and it becomes smaller, cuter, or more affectionate. Amor becomes amorzinho, café becomes cafezinho (that little coffee Brazilians love), a name like Ana becomes Aninha. This diminutive is everywhere in Brazil and instantly warms up your speech. Use it and you sound less like a textbook, more like a person, exactly the vibe of real Brazilian slang.

A quick note on tone

Context is everything. Amor and querida from a stranger can be totally friendly in Brazil, where warmth is normal, but read the room. Among friends and loved ones, though, pile them on. This warmth is the same emotional register as saudade, the untranslatable longing Brazilians feel so deeply.

The takeaway

Terms of endearment are the warmth of Brazilian Portuguese made audible. Learn amor, meu bem and the -inho trick, use them freely with the people you care about, and you instantly sound more Brazilian and more human. Ready to keep going? Frequently asked questions

What are common terms of endearment in Brazilian Portuguese?

Amor (love), meu bem (my dear), querido/querida (darling), benzinho (sweetie) and gato/gata (handsome/gorgeous) are among the most common.

What does meu bem mean?

Literally "my good," it means "my dear" or "sweetheart." It is a soft, affectionate term used with partners and loved ones.

Why do Brazilians add -inho to words?

The -inho/-inha diminutive makes a word smaller, cuter or more affectionate: amorzinho, cafezinho. It is a core part of warm, natural Brazilian speech. See the [slang guide](/speak-like-yourself/blog/brazilian-portuguese-slang/).
